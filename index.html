Summary

Modern research has become increasingly data-intensive, with fields such as bioinformatics generating vast amounts of raw data. Turning this data into meaningful results relies on large, complex analysis pipelines, making effective data management and integration central to scientific discovery. At the same time, growing awareness of the reproducibility crisis has highlighted the need to build these pipelines in a more reproducible way, since science advances by reproducing and reusing prior work. Scientific workflows, and in particular code-based systems such as Nextflow and Snakemake, have emerged as a solution to provide better reproducibility, offering software encapsulation, scalability and flexibility. Reusing workflows lets researchers build on existing components and supports collaborative and interdisciplinary research. However, workflows are often complex and defined in heterogeneous ways, which makes them hard to share, understand and discover, ultimately limiting their reuse. To provide an element of solution to this problem, this PhD work proposes a framework that: (i) models heterogeneous workflows as a homogeneous graph structure, bridging the implicit structures of code-based implementations and explicit workflow representations; (ii) formalises graph-based methods to automatically generate simplified representations of workflows at multiple levels of granularity, reducing their perceived complexity; and (iii) applies information visualisation principles to present these structures with a consistent visual metaphor, enabling interactive exploration across simplification levels and linking visuals directly to the source code. These solutions have been implemented in multiple open-source tools, making them directly available to the research community. Together, these contributions, at the intersection of data management and integration, visualisation and open-source software development, aim to support the overarching goal of enhancing workflow transparency and understandability, in order to facilitate workflow reuse.
